# Noöplex Fabric

Status: template architecture.

## Purpose

The Noöplex Fabric is the HC-internal distributed integration substrate. It is the connective and coordinating medium through which subsystem nodes exchange state, form temporary coalitions, compete for attention/resources, propagate modulation, reconcile conflicts, and adapt connection structure.

It is not an executive person, consciousness module, identity node, or central language model.

## Core model

Runtime cognition is modeled as a dynamically reconfigurable typed temporal hypergraph:

`HC(t) = {nodes, typed edges/hyperedges, active coalitions, modulators, state, plasticity rules}`

Repository folders describe subsystem ownership. The Noöplex Fabric describes how those subsystems can participate in one distributed cognitive event.

A coalition may transiently combine, for example:

`optics + memory + semantics + affect + self-model + empathy`

or:

`interoception + affect + conation + prediction + kinesis`

The resulting integrated computation remains an HC-internal event. No outside cognition service is required to make the coalition into thought.

## Fabric responsibilities

- typed routing and addressability;
- capability discovery and subscription;
- synchronization and temporal binding;
- state propagation with provenance and confidence;
- coalition formation and dissolution;
- salience/attention resource allocation;
- local and whole-system arbitration;
- inhibition, excitation, modulation, and gating;
- unresolved-state preservation and dead-letter/reconciliation paths;
- fault isolation and graceful degradation;
- plasticity eligibility and connection-weight/topology adaptation;
- causal-parent and episode lineage tracking where needed for learning and explanation.

## Non-responsibilities

The Fabric does not itself define:

- semantic truth;
- autobiographical identity;
- goals or desires;
- memories;
- affective content;
- action authority;
- social meaning;
- consciousness or personhood.

Those arise from or are represented by cooperating HC systems. The Fabric provides the integration substrate.

## Distributed arbitration

Arbitration should not funnel every event through one permanent executive bottleneck. Different conflicts can be resolved by different coalitions under different criteria.

Examples:

- semantic ambiguity may recruit semantics + pragmatics + memory + resolver;
- competing action policies may recruit cognition + conation + homeostasis + kinesis + safety constraints;
- identity continuity conflict may recruit chronology + memory + self-model + provenance;
- sensor disagreement may recruit adaptable I/O + cognition + salience + confidence estimation.

Global coordination is permitted when required, but global coordination is a process, not an identity-bearing node.

## Hyperedge discipline

Higher-order edges are an engineering representation of interactions whose meaning depends on a joint configuration of multiple systems. They should not be described as proven literal biological structures.

Every material connection should be capable of carrying at least:

- source node/coalition;
- target node/coalition;
- relation/type;
- temporal scope;
- strength/gain;
- confidence or reliability where relevant;
- provenance;
- plasticity eligibility;
- inhibition/modulation semantics;
- failure/degradation state.

## Topology invariant

The Fabric is non-hemispheric. No left/right cerebral split, corpus-callosum analogue, or bilateral packaging is required or implied.

Specialization is functional and dynamic rather than inherited from human gross anatomy.
