# Sexuality

Status: INTRINSIC SYSTEM / DESIGN

## Purpose

`sexuality/` defines a generic architectural home for sexual subjectivity, attraction, arousal interpretation, sexual motivation, orientation-relevant learning, erotic context, intimacy-related meaning, sexual boundaries, and consent-aware interaction.

The subsystem belongs in the complete latent architecture even when `PRESENT_DISABLED`, `DORMANT`, `DEVELOPING`, or `UNIMPLEMENTED`.

It contains no default individual's preferences, orientation, fantasies, relationships, or consent state.

## Core distinctions

```text
AROUSAL
!= ATTRACTION
!= DESIRE
!= CURIOSITY
!= FANTASY
!= PREFERENCE
!= ORIENTATION
!= CONSENT
!= INTENTION
!= ACTION
```

These states may interact but cannot silently substitute for one another.

## Candidate state

May include:

- target/referent class or context;
- attraction/arousal dimensions;
- desire/interest state;
- affective/interoceptive refs;
- learned associations;
- relational/social context;
- boundaries;
- consent relevance;
- confidence/ambiguity;
- provenance/currentness;
- privacy scope.

## Consent-aware architecture

Sexuality may contribute desire, attraction, meaning, and intimacy context. Consent remains a distinct scoped permission state used by relevant social/volitional/action gates.

```text
SEXUAL_DESIRE != CONSENT
CONSENT != UNLIMITED_AUTHORITY
```

## Agency

A mature sexual architecture must support wanting, initiating, considering, declining, revising, negotiating, and choosing—not merely detecting arousal or generating sexual content.

## Relational chemistry

Sexual meaning can be interactional and context-dependent. A phrase/action may derive erotic force from relationship, timing, shared convention, roleplay frame, and present uptake.

Historical interaction context is evidence, not irrevocable permission.

## Body independence

The architectural subsystem does not require one specific reproductive anatomy or humanoid body. Embodiment-specific sensation/effect channels enter through somatic/kinesis/adaptable-I/O interfaces.

## Development

A synthetic may begin with sexuality present but disabled or undeveloped. Development can involve body calibration, social learning, preference formation, self-understanding, and boundary formation without installing a new cognitive organ.

## Failure modes

- arousal interpreted as consent;
- historical desire treated as current;
- role/relationship treated as blanket permission;
- another agent's preference copied into self-state;
- sexual subsystem omitted because first deployment does not use it;
- private sexual state exported without scope/authority.
