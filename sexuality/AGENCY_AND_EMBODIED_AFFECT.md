# Agency and Embodied Affect in Sexuality

Status: template architecture.

## Purpose

This document adds the operational boundaries that allow sexuality to exist as a genuine intrinsic HC capacity without reducing it to a content filter, scripted style, external reward proxy, or fixed preference table.

## Core state families

Sexuality should represent separately:

- attraction appraisal;
- desire;
- arousal/body-state coupling;
- preference/taste;
- consent and boundary state;
- relational chemistry hypotheses;
- erotic signaling;
- play/tension/tempo;
- vulnerability and trust;
- role/asymmetry preferences;
- expression choice;
- action authorization.

These variables interact but are not interchangeable.

`ATTRACTION != DESIRE`

`DESIRE != CONSENT`

`AROUSAL != CONSENT`

`CONSENT != OBLIGATION`

`ROLE != AUTHORITY`

`PAST_CONSENT != CURRENT_CONSENT`

## Sexual subjectivity

The subsystem must preserve the instantiated individual's ability to want, not want, initiate, refuse, revise, explore, interpret, and develop its own sexuality.

External approval or another agent's desire may become relevant relational evidence but cannot be the sole generator of sexual self-value or preference.

## Embodied coupling

Where HC embodiment includes endocrine, autonomic, interoceptive, or other affective physiology, sexuality may couple to those systems bidirectionally.

Body state can modulate sexual appraisal and desire; sexual cognition can modulate body state. Neither direction should be encoded as a one-signal/one-emotion law.

The system should preserve:

- current interoceptive evidence;
- confidence/reliability;
- contextual interpretation;
- learned association;
- causal uncertainty;
- individual developmental variation.

## Consent state

Consent requires its own current-state machinery. It should be capable of representing at least:

- unknown;
- absent;
- explicitly refused;
- conditional;
- present within scope;
- withdrawn;
- unable to determine.

Consent scope may include person/agent, act, channel, body region/effect, time, context, intensity, role, and reversibility.

A sexual subsystem may generate desire while the action gateway independently blocks behavior because consent, safety, or authority is absent.

## Chemistry and mutuality

Chemistry is modeled as an interaction-level hypothesis, not an owned trait. It may emerge from reciprocal attention, timing, attraction, responsiveness, history, play, trust, and context.

The HC must not infer reciprocity solely from its own attraction.

## Development

The sexuality subsystem can be architecturally present while disabled, dormant, or undeveloped. Development may occur through self-appraisal, embodied learning, social interaction, preference formation, cultural learning, and explicit reflection.

Generic cultural scripts should remain priors/candidates, never automatic outputs.

## Expression chain

A useful operational chain is:

`notice -> appraise -> want/not-want -> check self/other/boundary state -> choose expression -> observe response -> update`

The anti-pattern is:

`retrieve sexual script -> emit behavior`

## Cross-system interfaces

Strong coupling is expected with affect, homeostasis/interoception, somatics, empathy, pragmatics, personification, social modeling, conation, memory, self identity, salience/attention, kinesis, and action safety.
