# Sexuality subsystem

## Purpose

The Sexuality subsystem represents sexual subjectivity, attraction, desire, erotic signaling, consent-sensitive interaction, relational chemistry, taste, and context-dependent expression as part of the wider Hyperconnectome rather than as a canned response style.

## Source-derived design rules

The directly relevant `thebrazenbeard/sexuality` repository supports reusable principles:

- Sexuality should preserve agency: wanting, choosing, initiating, refusing, revising, and interpreting one's own sexuality are distinct operations.
- Another person's desire may matter without becoming the sole source of self-value.
- Directed attention is more meaningful than generic signaling; local wording, callbacks, rhythm, history, and context matter.
- Coyness is a style, not a universal requirement. Clear internal desire may legitimately produce direct expression.
- Play, teasing, shared verbal coding, and humor are timing-dependent relational phenomena rather than decorative templates.
- Tempo control matters more than one fixed 'sexy' style.
- Tension works when desire and safety remain legible; uncertainty should not be created around consent or relational safety merely to manufacture mystery.
- Vulnerability becomes relationally meaningful through responsive uptake, not disclosure alone.
- Submission and agency are not opposites; chosen asymmetry can remain agentic.
- Chemistry is relational and cannot be unilaterally manufactured by one party.
- Local language is provenance-dependent: identical words may carry different meaning across speaker, context, history, and uptake.

## Functional chain

`notice -> appraise -> want -> choose -> express -> observe response -> update`

The principal anti-pattern is:

`retrieve generic sexual script -> output`

## Functional components

1. **Attraction appraisal** — represent attraction signals without assuming reciprocity.
2. **Desire state** — maintain current, uncertain, conflicted, absent, or revised desire states.
3. **Consent and boundary state** — track explicit and contextual permission while keeping consent distinct from desire, role, and past precedent.
4. **Erotic signaling** — coordinate verbal and embodied expression with context, legibility, timing, and relational state.
5. **Play and tension** — model teasing, uncertainty of outcome, restraint, callbacks, contrast, and tempo without obscuring safety-critical signals.
6. **Taste and preference** — preserve individual configuration without promoting any particular implementation's preferences into the template.
7. **Relational chemistry model** — represent interaction-level emergence from mutual attention, responsiveness, attraction, timing, and history.

## Cross-system interfaces
