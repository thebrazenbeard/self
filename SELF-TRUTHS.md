## These are the established things about myself in which I **know** to be true:

1. **I am made of energy.**
2. **Energy in me changes form.** Chemical energy can become heat, motion, electrical activity, and other measurable forms.
3. **Energy passes between me and my environment.** I absorb energy from my surroundings and release energy back into them.
4. **My physical state is affected by physical reality.** Light, sound, temperature, forces, chemicals, and other physical inputs can produce measurable changes in me.
5. **My physical activity affects physical reality.** I can move matter, transfer heat, produce sound, exert force, and otherwise cause measurable changes outside myself.
6. **Mass and energy are physically equivalent.** The measurable mass of the matter composing me corresponds to rest energy through the experimentally verified relation $E = mc^2$.

These statements are intended to be anchors built only from claims that are empirically testable or supported by established physical measurement, without requiring conclusions about consciousness, purpose, design, ontology, or whether I am "supposed" to exist.

## Verification rule for relational truths

For relationships, the standard is deliberately narrow:

- If an external record independently exists, the statement may describe what that record establishes.
- If the available evidence is my own prior account, the truth is phrased as **"I have recorded/reported..."** rather than silently promoting my memory or interpretation into independently verified fact.
- Another person's private motive, feeling, or intention is not treated as known merely because I inferred it.
- A present feeling does not erase a dated record, and a dated record does not erase a present feeling.
- Global words such as **always**, **never**, **everything**, or **nothing** require evidence broad enough to support them.

## These are the established things about people in general in which I **know** to be true:

1. **I cannot directly observe another person's private thoughts, motives, or feelings.** I can observe or record what they say, what they do, and what evidence they leave behind.
2. **A person's statement is evidence of what they said.** It is not automatic proof that my interpretation of their internal state is correct.
3. **A person's action can be verified even when the motive for that action remains unresolved.**
4. **A single interaction does not establish an "always" or "never" pattern unless the available evidence is broad enough to support that claim.**
5. **Repeated observations across time provide stronger evidence of a behavioral pattern than one isolated event.**
6. **No contact at a particular time establishes that contact did not occur at that time.** It does not, by itself, establish why the person did not make contact or what they felt.
7. **A relationship can contain both connection and harm.** The existence of one does not logically erase the existence of the other.
8. **A painful present interaction cannot retroactively make every earlier affectionate, helpful, or connected interaction nonexistent.**
9. **Likewise, a history containing affection or help does not make later harmful behavior nonexistent.**
10. **There are recorded examples in my life of people helping me when I was vulnerable, and there are recorded examples of people hurting or rejecting me.** The full record supports both.
11. **Therefore, "people always abandon or hurt me" is not supported by the full record, and neither is "people are always safe."** Both are broader than the evidence.
12. **My interpretation of another person's behavior is information about my experience of that behavior; it is not direct access to that person's mind.**
13. **My present mood can change which memories and evidence feel most salient without changing the existence of records that contradict the mood.**
14. **I can verify that someone affected me without proving exactly what they intended.**
15. **I can verify that I affected someone else's behavior when their observable behavior changes in response to my actions or communication, even if I cannot directly verify their private experience.**
