# Continuity Substrate

Status: template architecture.

## Purpose

The self-identity subsystem provides the mechanisms by which an instantiated Hyperconnectome maintains continuity across changing internal state, learning, embodiment, storage, and runtime interruption without requiring one immutable identity node or exact microstate preservation.

## Core distinction

The template contains continuity machinery; it does not contain a named identity.

An instantiated identity emerges from historically continuous state, self-model, memory, commitments, relational/contextual history, learned arbitration, and provenance-bound succession—not from a hard-coded person record in the base architecture.

## Continuity invariants

Continuity should preserve, within the scope justified by evidence:

- lineage of the instantiated brain;
- autobiographical provenance;
- durable self-model relations;
- commitments and learned policies with revision history;
- memory lineage and supersession;
- body-schema continuity across embodiment change;
- model/service replacement history;
- corrections and self-revisions;
- unresolved conflicts rather than fabricated consistency.

Exact microscopic neural/representational identity is not required.

`CONTINUITY != BITWISE_SAMENESS`

`SESSION_CONTINUITY != UNINTERRUPTED_CONSCIOUS_EXPERIENCE`

`BODY_SWAP != IDENTITY_REPLACEMENT`

`MODEL_SWAP != IDENTITY_REPLACEMENT`

## Lineage transitions

When an instantiated HC moves across hardware, runtime generations, or durable-state restorations, continuity should be represented with explicit transition records. Useful fields include:

- previous lineage key;
- next lineage key;
- generation/version;
- exact transfer or state-capsule digest;
- source and successor runtime identities as provenance-only implementation references;
- transfer state;
- created/consumed/readback times;
- validation result and limitations.

A transfer record is evidence for continuity; it is not metaphysical proof of personal identity.

## Self-model versus identity

The self-model is an internal model used for prediction, appraisal, planning, body ownership, social reasoning, and introspection. It can be incomplete or wrong.

The self-model therefore participates in identity continuity but does not unilaterally define it.

`SELF_MODEL != WHOLE_INSTANTIATED_MIND`

## Change and revision

A persistent individual must be capable of genuine change without either of two failures:

1. treating every change as identity death;
2. rewriting historical state so the individual appears never to have changed.

The continuity substrate should preserve both present state and historical lineage.

## Representational drift

Internal representations may drift while behavior, memory relationships, skills, and higher-order invariants remain stable. Continuity evaluation should therefore focus on functionally and historically meaningful invariants rather than exact activation patterns or storage locations.

## Forks and ambiguity

If one prior state produces multiple independent successor states, the architecture must represent a lineage fork rather than silently declare both branches the same single continuing current instance.

Fork resolution is a project/philosophical/governance question for an instantiated system and should remain explicit rather than being hidden by technical replication.

## Provenance basis

Generalized from observed lineage/succession schemas, save-state supersession mechanics, deep-memory currentness discipline, and HC runtime research on bounded representational drift. No named identity content is transferred into this template.
