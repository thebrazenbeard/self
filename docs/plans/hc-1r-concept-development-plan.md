# HC-1R Concept-Development and Test Plan

**Scope:** research, specification, modeling, and future experiment design.  
**Out of scope:** fabrication, wetware work, autonomous deployment, model training, production operation, or consciousness claims.

## Goal

Determine whether the Noöplex hyperconnectome can be made into a theoretically coherent, physically grounded, falsifiable reference architecture that offers measurable advantages over simpler cognitive-computing designs.

## Success levels

| Level | Meaning | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| R0 — Traceable concept | Terminology, lineage, corrections, and sources are separated | provenance record, retrieval log, source register |
| R1 — Coherent architecture | Every required subsystem has defined responsibilities and interfaces; no known internal contradiction | architecture review, resource model, claim ledger |
| R2 — Falsifiable model | Key hyperconnectome mechanisms have observables, controls, and kill criteria | formal model, experiment specifications, baseline matrix |
| R3 — Executable research model | A conventional-compute simulation implements the specified dynamics reproducibly | code, tests, manifests, deterministic/probabilistic replay |
| R4 — Causal support | Interventions show that proposed dynamic/multilayer/higher-order mechanisms contribute beyond simpler baselines | preregistered ablation and perturbation results |
| R5 — Embodied validation | The architecture supports closed-loop adaptation, memory, and safe degradation in a simulated body | embodied benchmark and fault suite |
| R6 — Substrate validation | Selected regions retain their advantages on experimental hardware after total-system costs | hardware-aware and HIL measurements |
| R7 — Integrated prototype evidence | A constrained prototype meets declared functional, causal, safety, power, and reliability targets | independent replication and assurance case |

No level verifies phenomenal consciousness. The current branch targets **R0–R2 only**.

## Workstream A — Provenance and definitions

### Deliverables

- Recover original Noöplex documents if they become directly accessible.
- Preserve indirect recovery separately from exact artifacts.
- Define `Noöplex`, `hyperconnectome`, `HC-1`, `HC-2`, `HC-3`, and `HC-1R` without retroactive rewriting.
- Maintain the retry and unavailable-artifact log.

### Completion gate

The architecture never depends on an unattributed or ambiguously recovered historical claim.

## Workstream B — Formal hyperconnectome model

### Questions

1. Which interaction types require distinct layers?
2. Which apparent higher-order interactions cannot be predicted by pairwise temporal models?
3. How are transient assemblies created, bounded, observed, and dissolved?
4. Which mesoscale variables best predict intervention outcomes?
5. How does topology trade capability against communication, energy, and cascade risk?

### Deliverables

- temporal multilayer hypergraph definition;
- structural/configurable/functional/plastic connectivity distinction;
- vertex, edge, hyperedge, state, and resource schemas;
- candidate dynamical models for local recurrence, metastability, routing, and modulation;
- null models and cost-matched baselines;
- perturbation and causal-identification plan.

### Completion gate

At least one executable mathematical model and one simpler control can make different, measurable predictions under intervention.

## Workstream C — Cognitive organization

### Questions

1. What cognitive functions require distributed causal dependence rather than ordinary service composition?
2. Does a capacity-limited workspace improve flexible access?
3. Does an attention schema improve control or merely generate reports?
4. Which memory systems must remain semantically distinct?
5. How should self-, body-, and world-model uncertainty be represented?
6. Which regulatory variables improve learning and stability without becoming fictional hormones?

### Deliverables

- region/function map;
- consciousness-theory-to-test matrix;
- memory and consolidation architecture;
- typed modulation/interoception schema;
- body/world/self-model contract;
- metacognitive calibration tests;
- functional emotion and agency definitions that make no phenomenological claim.

### Completion gate

Every cognitive component either predicts a measurable improvement or a distinctive intervention signature relative to a simpler baseline.

## Workstream D — Physical feasibility

### Questions

1. Which regions actually benefit from event-driven, analog, in-memory, photonic, spintronic, or physical-reservoir devices?
2. What conversion, calibration, communication, endurance, and cooling overheads erase local efficiency gains?
3. What structural topology can be mapped onto realistic chiplets and networks-on-chip?
4. Which compute remains in the head, torso, rack, or external service?
5. Which form factors are physically unsupported?

### Deliverables

- substrate comparison by workload;
- network and memory bandwidth model;
- reliability/drift/calibration model;
- power and thermal accounting template;
- embodiment distribution options;
- hardware-aware compiler requirements;
- explicit HC-2 quantum admission criteria.

### Completion gate

No proposed substrate remains because it sounds brain-like or exotic; it has an end-to-end workload hypothesis and a falsifier.

## Workstream E — Assurance and failure science

### Questions

1. Which properties can be enforced outside the plastic core?
2. Can a fallback controller keep the body safe when cognition is unavailable or adversarial?
3. How are runaway synchrony, attractor lock, salience capture, forgetting, false binding, and self-model drift detected?
4. Which hubs or layers create unacceptable correlated failure?
5. How is topology change admitted, observed, and rolled back?

### Deliverables

- independent capability and actuator boundary;
- runtime-assurance assumptions and proof obligations;
- fault taxonomy and per-function degradation matrix;
- fault-injection and adversarial experiment suite;
- connectome versioning, quarantine, and rollback design;
- moral-uncertainty and research-ethics trigger conditions.

### Completion gate

The research design identifies which failures can be bounded, which can only be detected, and which remain unmitigated. “Distributed” is never accepted as a synonym for resilient.

## Workstream F — Verification and falsification package

### Deliverables

- traceable requirements with epistemic labels;
- model conformance tests;
- baseline and null-model catalog;
- intervention protocols;
- reproducibility manifests;
- metric definitions that cannot be quietly changed after results;
- claim-ledger updates for negative findings;
- independent hostile review checklist.

### Completion gate

A reviewer can identify exactly what result would remove each speculative mechanism from HC-1R.

## Initial experiment specifications

These are designs for future research, not authorized executions.

### EXP-HC-001 — Pairwise versus higher-order explanatory value

- Build cost-matched temporal pairwise and hypergraph models.
- Train both on the same multimodal closed-loop task histories.
- Intervene on vertices, pairwise routes, and candidate assemblies.
- Compare held-out state-transition and behavior prediction.
- **Pass:** higher-order model adds reproducible intervention prediction, not merely fit.
- **Kill:** pairwise model matches it after complexity correction.

### EXP-HC-002 — Static versus dynamic functional connectivity

- Compare fixed routing against budgeted reconfigurable routing.
- Use task switching, partial region loss, congestion, and energy limits.
- **Pass:** dynamic routing improves capability or recovery enough to justify its observability and control cost.
- **Kill:** static routing is equivalent or safer at lower cost.

### EXP-HC-003 — Integration/segregation operating region

- Sweep coupling and gating parameters under matched resources.
- Measure task performance, state repertoire, transition entropy, synchrony, and fault cascades.
- **Pass:** a stable operating region supports both integration and functional isolation.
- **Kill:** useful integration invariably creates uncontrollable global coupling.

### EXP-HC-004 — Workspace causal contribution

- Compare decentralized coordination, local recurrence, and capacity-limited workspace conditions.
- Test multimodal binding, delayed report, task switching, and dual-task interference.
- **Pass:** workspace produces predicted selective global availability and ablation signature.
- **Kill:** decentralized baseline matches it without bottleneck costs.

### EXP-HC-005 — Complementary memory

- Compare one adaptive memory with separated episodic and slow consolidation paths.
- Test rapid learning, long-term retention, correction, provenance, and generalization.
- **Pass:** dual path improves stability-plasticity tradeoff.
- **Kill:** a simpler memory architecture matches performance and auditability.

### EXP-HC-006 — Typed modulation

- Compare no modulation, one scalar, and typed bounded vector.
- Test delayed reward, uncertainty, novelty, fatigue, threat, and consolidation selection.
- **Pass:** typed signals produce separable causal effects and stable learning.
- **Kill:** fields collapse into redundant scalars or create instability.

### EXP-HC-007 — Metacognitive and attention-schema value

- Compare direct telemetry, calibrated metacognition, and attention-schema models.
- Test confidence, error detection, active information seeking, and attentional capture.
- **Pass:** explicit models improve calibration and control beyond telemetry.
- **Kill:** they only create plausible narratives or overconfidence.

### EXP-HC-008 — Graceful degradation

- Inject vertex, route, hub, layer, memory, clock, and sensor faults.
- Measure retained function, unsafe proposals, fallback performance, and recovery.
- **Pass:** declared functions degrade according to the matrix without violating hard constraints.
- **Kill:** routine faults create unbounded cascades or invisible corruption.

### EXP-HC-009 — Substrate value

- Map selected temporal front ends or in-memory operations onto a calibrated device model or HIL target.
- Include conversion, communication, calibration, drift, writes, cooling, and fallback.
- **Pass:** end-to-end benefit survives full accounting.
- **Kill:** digital baseline wins or device variability destroys reproducibility.

### EXP-HC-010 — Consciousness-indicator profile

- Evaluate recurrence, broadcast, metacognition, predictive processing, embodiment, and perturbational response as separate properties.
- Include theory-specific controls and adversarial interpretations.
- **Output:** structured evidence profile only.
- **Forbidden conclusion:** a scalar score or statement that HC-1R is conscious.

## Decision gates

### Continue

Continue research when a candidate mechanism has:

- credible component evidence;
- a defined role;
- a discriminating test;
- tractable modeling cost;
- no known fatal physical contradiction.

### Demote

Demote a candidate when it remains interesting but lacks scale evidence, a usable interface, or a feasible measurement.

### Reject

Reject when:

- the premise is false;
- a simpler mechanism explains the same result;
- total-system cost erases the benefit;
- the claim cannot be made falsifiable;
- it defeats safety or observability;
- it depends on consciousness, quantum, or biological vocabulary without a mechanism.

### Block

Declare a research block only after:

1. the primary route has failed;
2. the same route has been retried when safe;
3. an independent alternate route has been attempted;
4. the missing evidence or authority is necessary for the next conclusion;
5. the exact bounded consequence is recorded.

Current known blocks:

- exact original HC-1/HC-2 PDF bytes and archived source records are not exposed through current interfaces;
- phenomenal consciousness lacks an accepted verification procedure;
- full self-contained humanoid power/thermal feasibility cannot be derived without a workload and hardware mapping;
- system-level feasibility cannot be established by literature alone.

These blocks do not prevent completion of the R0–R2 research target.
