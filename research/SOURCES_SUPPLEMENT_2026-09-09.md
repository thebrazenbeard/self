## Source-use cautions

1. Human sexual-neuroscience literature has major construct and paradigm limitations; desire, arousal, motivation, genital response, attraction and partner specificity must not be treated as interchangeable merely because studies use overlapping stimuli or regions.
2. Distributed neural correlates do not imply one-to-one architectural modules in a synthetic system.
3. `Wanting`, `liking`, effort, learning and action selection are useful dissociations; they do not define a complete universal theory of synthetic motivation.
4. Biological sexual-response evidence can constrain a synthetic analogue, but it does not establish that a computational state has human sexual phenomenology.
5. Self-representation theories and bodily-self studies constrain plausible synthetic self-modeling patterns but do not establish that a computational self-model is a complete self or that it entails subjective self-awareness.
6. Developmental self-model proposals are useful design pressure, not instructions to reproduce human ontogeny literally.
